01 / The challenge

What if footwear could do more?

Our team explored how Nike could move beyond one-time product transactions toward connected, personalized, and ongoing customer relationships — while evaluating the technology investments and operational risks that transformation would require.

Working as HypnoTech Consulting LLC, a four-person academic team, we developed a proposal spanning product design, customer experience, new data sources, financial projections, and change management.

02 / The strategy

Four connected ideas, one customer journey

PILLAR 01

Connected footwear · IoT

Explore sensors embedded in shoes to capture activity and performance signals, sync insights to an app, and support personalized feedback.

Business opportunity: richer product feedback and customer engagement.

PILLAR 02

Adaptive footwear · 3D/4D

Investigate thermochromic and photochromic materials, plus shape-memory polymers or alloys, for footwear that could respond to environmental changes.

Business opportunity: product differentiation, customization, and material innovation.

PILLAR 03

Nike Perks · Subscription

Propose a monthly footwear rental model, rotating styles, customer preference profiles, and logistics partnerships to support recurring engagement.

Business opportunity: recurring revenue and demand insights.

PILLAR 04

Shopping app · AI + AR

Conceptualize a redesigned mobile experience with conversational product recommendations, personalized discovery, and virtual try-on.

Business opportunity: more intuitive discovery and tailored shopping.

These are proposed concepts from the 2023 academic report, not confirmed technical capabilities or launches.

03 / My contribution

Where I focused my work

Our team divided the research and writing by topic. My documented contributions were 3D/4D printing strategy, projected financial impact, and risks and mitigations.

For the adaptive footwear concept, I researched how smart materials might support color-changing or shape-responsive products, and identified potential research and manufacturing collaborators for further exploration.

For the business case, I developed a phased financial narrative supported by Tableau dashboards, considering revenue categories, cost of sales, operating expenses, and property, plant, and equipment.

My workstreams

  • Technology assessment: 3D/4D printing applications and possible partnerships
  • Financial scenario modeling: projected revenue, gross profit, and expense trends
  • Risk strategy: data privacy, investment requirements, and adoption challenges

06 / Risk & responsibility

Innovation needs guardrails

Privacy & security

Connected products and personalization create sensitive data risks. We recommended layered security, authentication, encryption, and dedicated oversight.

Capital investment

New hardware, research, logistics, and platforms require significant spending. A phased approach would allow investment decisions to respond to early results.

Adoption & change

Employees and customers may not immediately embrace unfamiliar offerings. Training, communication, feedback, and iterative pilots were essential.

07 / Recommendations

From vision to validation

01 · Research

Test market demand, technical feasibility, vendor suitability, and financial assumptions.

02 · Pilot

Start with limited products or audiences, measure outcomes, and learn before scaling.

03 · Plan

Set milestones, owners, success measures, and a cross-functional change-management approach.

09 / Reflection

What this project taught me

This project challenged me to think like a consultant: not simply to propose exciting technologies, but to ask whether they solve a customer problem, create business value, and can be implemented responsibly.

Working on the 4D printing proposal showed me how much research is needed to move from an intriguing concept to a feasible product. Building the financial projections helped me connect strategic ambition with costs, investment timing, and measurable assumptions. The risk analysis also reminded me that cybersecurity, privacy, and organizational readiness are central to any transformation.

Looking back, the biggest lesson I learned was that digital transformation is as much about people, economics, and execution as it is about technology.
