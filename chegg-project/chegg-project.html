01 / The challenge

When answers become free, what makes learning valuable?

As generative AI tools made instant answers widely accessible, our team explored how Chegg could move beyond traditional homework assistance and create a more differentiated learning experience built on trust, mentorship, and academic growth.

Our three-person consulting team examined Chegg's competitive pressure, subscription challenges, pricing, brand perception, and the adoption barriers facing its AI offering, CheggMate. The goal was to identify strategic opportunities rather than claim that our proposals had been adopted.

02 / Strategic diagnosis

Understanding the pressure on Chegg

CHALLENGE 01

Free AI alternatives

General-purpose AI tools offered fast responses at little or no cost, putting pressure on a paid homework-answer model.

Strategic question: What value can Chegg offer beyond answer speed?

CHALLENGE 02

Subscription pressure

Our presentation cited a 23% year-over-year decline in subscription revenue, highlighting pressure on Chegg's existing model.

Strategic question: How could Chegg rebuild willingness to pay?

CHALLENGE 03

Trust & academic integrity

Students and educators face uncertainty around the reliability and appropriate use of AI-assisted academic work.

Strategic question: Can learning support become a source of differentiation?

CHALLENGE 04

Unclear AI value

Our team considered whether CheggMate's positioning, perceived benefits, and pricing could limit mainstream adoption.

Strategic question: How can Chegg make its value proposition easier to understand?

03 / The strategy

From a red ocean to a blue ocean

Using Blue Ocean Strategy, we contrasted competing for faster, cheaper answers with creating a more distinctive experience around meaningful learning, personalization, and mentorship.

RED OCEAN · CURRENT COMPETITION

Competing on answers

Emphasis on quick homework solutions, price, and content volume in a crowded market with free alternatives.

Risk: limited differentiation as automated answers become commonplace.

BLUE OCEAN · PROPOSED OPPORTUNITY

Competing on learning outcomes

Emphasis on verified guidance, personalized study support, human mentorship, and responsible AI assistance.

Opportunity: create reasons to choose Chegg beyond speed alone.

04 / Four actions framework

Redesigning the value proposition

ACTION 01

Eliminate

Reduce reliance on undifferentiated, traditional answer-only experiences that free AI can easily replicate.

Intended shift: from passive answer delivery to active academic engagement.

ACTION 02

Reduce

Address confusing pricing and unnecessary friction that can discourage students from choosing a paid service.

Intended shift: simpler, more transparent value.

ACTION 03

Raise

Prioritize academic integrity, trust, and meaningful learning rather than optimizing solely for instant solutions.

Intended shift: more credible, education-centered support.

ACTION 04

Create

Explore a hybrid AI-and-human study coach combining personalized guidance with tutor support.

Intended shift: mentorship and learning experiences that stand apart.

05 / Technology adoption

Crossing the gap to mainstream learners

Our team also used the Innovation Adoption Curve to examine CheggMate. In our presentation, we placed it in an early-adopter phase and discussed the challenge of moving toward the early majority. This was a qualitative interpretation, not a measured adoption statistic.

We identified several possible barriers: limited trust in AI-generated answers, an unclear distinction from free alternatives, pricing concerns, and existing brand perceptions.

The lesson was that technological capability alone does not guarantee adoption. Users also need a clear reason to trust, understand, and repeatedly use the product.

Adoption barriers explored

  • Trust: confidence in AI-supported learning
  • Clarity: what CheggMate offers differently
  • Value: justification for a paid subscription
  • Perception: alignment with academic integrity

07 / Recommendations

From strategic idea to practical validation

NEXT STEP 01

Research student needs

Survey students about trust, mentorship, academic integrity, and willingness to pay.

NEXT STEP 02

Pilot hybrid support

Test small-scale AI-and-human tutoring concepts before considering wider rollout.

NEXT STEP 03

Test brand positioning

Use interviews and focus groups to understand how learners perceive Chegg relative to AI alternatives.

NEXT STEP 04

Evaluate risks

Explore legacy-user retention, AI trust, educator concerns, and feasibility before committing to a major shift.

These were proposed next steps in our academic presentation, not completed pilots or verified business outcomes.

09 / Reflection

What this project taught me

This project challenged me to look beyond a company's immediate competitive threats and ask whether it could redefine the value customers receive. Applying Blue Ocean Strategy made me think about creating meaningful differentiation instead of simply trying to outperform free AI tools on speed.

Studying technology adoption also reminded me that even promising innovations need user trust, a clear value proposition, and thoughtful timing. Combining the frameworks helped me connect strategic ambition with realistic adoption barriers and risks.

My biggest takeaway was that lasting innovation is not just about introducing new technology — it is about creating value that people genuinely understand and trust.
