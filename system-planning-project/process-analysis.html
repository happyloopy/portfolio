Retail Inventory & Systems Optimization

Business Process Modeling · Requirements Analysis · CRM · Feasibility Study · Systems Planning

How can a small retailer keep its in-store and online inventory consistent while reducing manual work and improving customer service? Our team analyzed existing retail workflows and proposed a cloud-based inventory, checkout, and CRM solution supported by process models and a feasibility study.

Project context. This was an academic systems analysis and implementation proposal, not a live software deployment. The written case uses the hypothetical name “ABC Vintage Store”; the executive presentation identifies the retail example as Doc’s Records. Operational improvements described below are proposed or estimated, not measured outcomes.

01 · The Business Challenge

The case centered on a vintage retailer selling records and collectibles through both a physical shop and an online storefront. Manual inventory updates created the risk of mismatched stock information, incomplete listings, delayed product updates, and lost customer confidence. The team also identified limited ways to capture customer preferences and feedback.

Current state

  • Manual inventory logging and periodic website updates
  • Potentially unavailable items still shown online
  • Staff-dependent checkout and inventory processes
  • Limited customer preference and feedback tracking

Proposed future state

  • Real-time inventory synchronization across channels
  • Barcode-supported receiving and stock updates
  • Self-service checkout options
  • Integrated customer history, feedback, and promotions

02 · Process Analysis & Requirements

We examined inventory receiving, sorting, online listings, checkout, and customer engagement. Our As-Is process model and customer-facing service blueprint helped locate manual steps and points where stock information could become outdated. We then created customer and employee personas and translated their needs into 12 user stories spanning inventory tracking, online visibility, CRM, and system integration.

The proposed To-Be swimlane model illustrated how scanning new arrivals, automatically synchronizing inventory, enabling self-checkout, and capturing purchase information could reduce repeated data entry and improve the shopping experience.

03 · Solution & Software Evaluation

Our proposed solution combined a cloud-based point-of-sale platform with inventory and CRM functionality. We compared Square and Vend using weighted criteria covering real-time tracking, website visibility, customer engagement, ease of employee entry, and software cost.

Square · Recommended

500 weighted points. The team favored Square for its integrated retail and online functionality, customer engagement tools, accessible interface, and lower quoted subscription cost in our historical comparison.

Vend · Alternative

390 weighted points. Vend also supported retail inventory management, but our evaluation scored it lower on several website, CRM, usability, and cost criteria.

Scores and vendor pricing reflect the team's original project-era assumptions and are not current product comparisons.

04 · Feasibility & Implementation Planning

We considered economic, technical, and operational feasibility, including subscriptions and equipment, staff training, compatibility with existing systems, cloud connectivity, data security, and employee adoption. The implementation plan proposed staged preparation, data transfer, user training, testing, rollout, and post-launch monitoring.

Estimated benefit, not an observed result: the report discussed a potential increase in checkout capacity through reduced bottlenecks and self-service options. Because its numerical capacity estimates are not fully consistent, this case study does not present them as verified performance gains.

06 · Reflection

This project strengthened my understanding of how systems planning begins with people and processes, not software. Building As-Is and To-Be models helped me see how small operational bottlenecks can affect both employee workload and customer experience. Creating user stories and evaluating competing platforms also showed me the importance of connecting technical features to business requirements, cost, feasibility, and change management.

My biggest takeaway was that recommending a system is only one part of improving a process. A realistic implementation plan also needs training, stakeholder alignment, risk mitigation, and a way to evaluate whether the proposed changes actually work.
